Rogers, Bass, and What Was Missing

Two foundational models have shaped how we understand technology diffusion for sixty years. Zorthex does not replace them. It measures something they were not designed to capture — and the gap turns out to matter.

The Three Models

Model 01
Rogers
Diffusion of Innovations · 1962
Qualitative and sociological. Describes who adopts and in what sequence — Innovators, Early Adopters, Early Majority, Late Majority, Laggards. Explains the decision process inside the organisation or individual. Does not predict timing.
Model 02
Bass
Diffusion Model · 1969
Mathematical and quantitative. Models the cumulative adoption curve using two coefficients: innovation (p) and imitation (q). Assumes gradual, continuous diffusion driven by word-of-mouth. Strong for forecasting consumer products.
Model 03
Zorthex
Diffusion Lag Framework · 2026
Empirical and descriptive. Measures the lag between when a phenomenon becomes operationally real and when public attention consolidates around it. 70 verified cases, three independent sources, modern digital proxies. Does not predict — classifies and measures.

What Each Model Actually Measures

Rogers — the process inside

Rogers is concerned with the individual or organisational adoption decision. He asks: why do some people adopt earlier than others? What attributes of the innovation matter? How does the social system shape uptake? Rogers works from the inside out — his unit of analysis is the adopter.

Bass — the aggregate curve

Bass translates Rogers's intuition into a mathematical model. He asks: given historical adoption data, can we fit a curve and forecast future uptake? His model works well for consumer goods with clear sales data — televisions, washing machines, smartphones. It assumes that adoption spreads gradually through imitation, following a smooth S-curve.

Zorthex — the visibility gap

Zorthex asks a different question: when does a technology become visible to the public? Not when it is invented. Not when it is adopted. When it generates sustained, structural attention — measured through modern digital proxies: Google Trends (operational signal), Wikipedia pageviews (informational signal), Reddit (community proxy).

The metric is L = t_peak − t_start: the gap in months between first documented emergence and structural public attention consolidation. Across 70 verified cases, this gap averages years, not months — and it varies systematically by domain and regime.

The key distinction: Rogers and Bass study diffusion — adoption spreading through a population. Zorthex studies attention diffusion — awareness spreading through public consciousness. They are measuring different phenomena. Both matter. Neither replaces the other.

Where Zorthex Challenges Bass

The Bass model rests on an assumption of gradual diffusion — adoption spreads continuously through imitation, producing a smooth S-curve. This works well for consumer products. It breaks down for a specific class of technologies.

The Dual-Velocity challenge to Bass: In B2B, security, cryptographic infrastructure, and policy-driven domains, the diffusion pattern is not gradual. Specialist attention (Wikipedia) runs at sustained high levels for years — sometimes decades — while public search attention (Google Trends) stays near zero. Then, triggered by a regulatory event, public attention ignites in a single month. The S-curve never forms. The breakout is vertical.

Bass's model would smooth this into a gradual curve. The data shows something different: a long invisible phase followed by a single-month ignition. The mechanism is not imitation — it is institutional legibility. When regulators move, the public suddenly has a frame for something specialists have known for years.

How the Three Models Fit Together

The models are not competitors. They answer different questions. Used together, they provide a more complete picture of how technologies move through societies and markets.

If you need to... Best model Why
Forecast sales or adoption volume Bass Mathematical formula, strong historical track record for consumer products
Understand which market segments to target first Rogers Explains who adopts early and what drives their decision
Know when a technology will become publicly visible Zorthex Measures the lag between operational reality and public attention with modern digital proxies
Navigate B2B, regulated, or policy-driven technologies Zorthex (Dual-Velocity) Shows that these often do not follow Bass's gradual curve — specialist layer activates years before the public trigger
Build a complete picture All three Rogers + Bass = adoption dynamics. Zorthex = timing of public visibility. Complementary, not alternative.
In summary: Rogers and Bass study the diffusion of adoption. Zorthex studies the diffusion of attention. For most consumer technologies, the two coincide closely enough that the distinction does not matter. For complex, regulated, and B2B technologies — where specialists operate for years before the public has a vocabulary — the gap between adoption and attention is measurable, systematic, and operationally significant. That is where Zorthex works.
