Part 1 — The Proof

Fifteen years of silence. Then three months of noise.

In December 2010, the U.S. Department of Energy published its first Critical Materials Strategy — a 190-page report documenting America's near-total dependence on Chinese rare earth processing for defense systems, electric vehicles, and wind turbines. The document was clear, public, and alarming.

Google Trends for "critical minerals" in the months following that report:

0
Google Trends score · every month · for fifteen years
December 2010 — July 2025. Zero searches. The report existed. The vulnerability existed. The public had no vocabulary for it.

The International Energy Agency published "The Role of Critical Minerals in Clean Energy Transitions" in May 2021. President Biden signed Executive Order 14017 on supply chain resilience in February 2021. The EU passed the Critical Raw Materials Act in 2024. China controlled 70% of rare earth mining and 90% of processing throughout.

Google Trends during all of this: 0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 1, 2, 2.

Then August 2025:

June 2025

July 2025
U.S.-MP Materials agreement: $400M preferred equity, $150M loan for heavy rare-earth separation, and — most significantly — a 10-year floor price at $110/kg for neodymium-praseodymium plus a 10-year magnet offtake. The first structural intervention in the market.

Score jumps to 30/100. The term crosses the structural threshold (25) for the first time in history.

The invisible companion

There is a second term that tells the same story with even more force. "Rare earth supply chain" — the term used by defense analysts, procurement officers, and industrial strategists — registered below 1/100 on Google Trends for fifteen consecutive years. Not below threshold. Below one. The entire specialist infrastructure — reports, strategies, procurement decisions, billions in contracts — operated in a space the public literally could not search for.

Then July 2025: 57. August: 73. October: 100.

The ratio of silence to explosion — fifteen years at <1, then 100 in three months — is the most extreme attention gap documented in the Zorthex dataset. More extreme than stablecoin sanctions (L=81 months at 0). More extreme than SMR (L=243 months but with a gradual rise). This was a vertical wall.

What this proves — and what it doesn't

The critical minerals case proves that a phenomenon can be fully documented, strategically critical, and actively managed by governments and institutions for fifteen years while the public search intent for it remains at zero. The specialist layer operates. The public layer doesn't know the name.

It does not prove that the breakout was predictable in timing. It proves that the gap was measurable — and that the gap, while open, represented an informational asymmetry with direct financial consequences.

MP Materials (NYSE: MP) traded at ~$12 in September 2024, when "critical minerals" scored 0/100. It reached ~$35 in October 2025, when the score hit 100. The price moved when the public moved. Not before, not after.

Why the breakout happened — the binding agent

The reports existed. The laws existed. The vulnerability existed. What was missing was what former Australian Prime Minister Scott Morrison — who in 2019 signed the first U.S.-Australia critical minerals supply chain agreement — calls the "binding agent" that industry and government needed to overcome China's grip on the market.

In a September 2026 op-ed in the Wall Street Journal, Morrison described what changed: China's decision to activate its leverage — export controls on gallium, germanium, graphite, antimony, tungsten, tellurium, and seven medium and heavy rare earths — forced the West to respond at scale.

"China changed the price of ignoring it, for government and industry alike. That is the binding agent China has given us."

— Scott Morrison, former Prime Minister of Australia, Wall Street Journal, September 2026

The institutional response that followed was not a spike. It was structural:

Floor prices and offtake agreements. The July 2025 MP Materials deal introduced a 10-year minimum floor price for neodymium-praseodymium at $110/kg, coupled with a 10-year magnet offtake. As Morrison notes, this "inoculated producers against China's price manipulation, ensuring their ventures remained commercially viable for investors" — a mechanism that, unlike a subsidy, cannot be easily reversed by market manipulation.

Allied scale. The U.S.-Australia framework mobilized at least $1B in October 2025, growing to $3.5B across 11 named projects by April 2026. A parallel U.S.-Japan framework added pricing measures, financing, offtake, stockpiling, and a rapid-response mechanism.

Strategic reserves. In February 2026, Project Vault — America's first strategic critical-minerals reserve for civilian industry — launched with $10B in Export-Import Bank financing plus nearly $2B in private capital.

These are not emergency measures. They are the architecture of a market that is being deliberately rebuilt. And they explain why the attention breakout of October 2025 was not a spike, but a signal of something durable.

Part 2 — How the Gap Is Measured

Zorthex measures Diffusion Lag (L): the interval, in months, between a phenomenon's first documented emergence and the consolidation of mainstream public attention. The measurement rests on three independent sources, each tracking a different cognitive stage:

Google Trends
Search Intent
When someone chooses to know. Not what people see — what they actively decide to look for, understand, and name. The moment of decision.
Wikipedia
Knowledge Consolidation
When specialists document. Article depth, edit frequency, cross-linking density. The institutional memory of a field. Editorially controlled, timestamped.
Reddit
Community Formation
When a group forms around the phenomenon. Subreddit creation, crossover from specialist to general forums. The social proof of emergence.

The gap between the three sources is the signal. When Wikipedia is high and Google Trends is low, specialists know but the public doesn't — the window is open. When Google Trends rises while Wikipedia stabilizes, the public is catching up — the window is closing. When all three converge, consolidation is complete.

Zorthex does not measure social media exposure or mentions. It measures search intent — the moment someone actively decides to look for, understand, and name a phenomenon. That is a fundamentally different signal: not what people see, but what they choose to know.

Reddit is used as a third verification source — not for exposure metrics, but for community formation: whether a dedicated group has formed around the phenomenon. This is structurally different from measuring how many people saw a post.

Part 4 — What This Means

The window is the product.

Every dataset in the alternative data market measures activity: who is buying, what is moving, where money flows. None of them measures the moment the rest of the world decides to pay attention. That moment — the transition from specialist knowledge to public search intent — is when prices move, regulations bite, and capital reallocates. It is measurable. It is verified. And it is, as of this writing, measured by no one else.

The critical minerals case is the proof. Fifteen years of government reports, institutional strategies, and billions in classified procurement — and zero public search intent until a geopolitical trigger made the invisible visible. The price of the primary U.S. rare earth company tripled in the months surrounding the breakout.
