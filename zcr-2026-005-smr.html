// Why the L Value Matters

The Zorthex diffusion lag metric L quantifies a window that institutional actors use implicitly but have never been able to measure: the interval between when something becomes operationally real and when public attention consolidates around it.

For SMR, that window was 243 months — over 20 years. During those 20 years, the IAEA published technical standards, NuScale obtained the only NRC design certification, TerraPower secured $650M from Bill Gates and NVIDIA, X-energy raised $700M from Amazon, and China built the world's first commercial land-based SMR. All of this happened while Google Trends showed 2–7 out of 100.

The breakout in August 2025 closed that window. It was triggered by a convergence of signals: AI data centre energy demand, DOE executive orders mandating three operational reactors by July 4, 2026, and $1.3 billion in equity investment in a single year. The public frame crystallised in a single month.

What this documents: The transition from discovery phase to execution phase. Before August 2025, SMR exposure was a contrarian infrastructure bet visible primarily to institutional and policy actors. After August 2025, it became a mainstream public narrative. The pricing environment, the competitive landscape, and the regulatory posture all shifted at that moment. L = 243 months documents exactly when.

This pattern — long institutional latency followed by sudden public consolidation — is consistent with other infrastructure transitions in the Zorthex dataset: Green Hydrogen (L=204), Cybersecurity (L=145), Zero Day Vulnerability (L=231). The regime shape is recognisable. When a new infrastructure phenomenon appears with a similar early profile — high institutional activity, near-zero public attention — the L values of documented precedents provide the regime context.

Rationale: First institutional document formalising SMR as a reactor category with defined parameters (≤300 MWe, modular design, factory-fabricated). This moment marks when SMR transitioned from individual research projects to a recognised institutional category with international standards.

// 06 · Declared Limitations

t_start alternative candidates. Four alternatives exist: 2001 (DOE conceptualisation, L=295), 2007 (NuScale founding, L=213), 2008 (first GT signal, L=209), 2012 (DOE SMR program, L=161). The 2005 choice reflects first institutional formalisation of the category. The rationale is declared and alternatives documented.
Query specificity. The query "small modular reactor" captures the generic category. Branded queries ("NuScale," "Oklo," "TerraPower") would produce different values and potentially earlier breakout dates. The US geographic score (20) is likely depressed by query fragmentation across brand names.
Wikipedia source. The Wikipedia page en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_modular_reactor is a well-established article with 684 views/day average. The signal is robust, though event-driven spikes (Oct 2024, Nov 2025) make monthly averages volatile.
August 2026. The current month (August 2026) shows 17/100 — this is a partial-month reading and is excluded from all calculations per Zorthex methodology. The STRUCTURAL classification is based on the 12 complete months August 2025 – July 2026.
