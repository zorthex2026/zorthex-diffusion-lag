Attention vs Adoption
Between and Beyond Bass and Rogers

More than seventy frameworks try to explain how technologies transform markets and societies. Most study adoption. None of them measure when a phenomenon becomes publicly visible. That is the gap Zorthex was built to fill.

The Three Core Models

Model 01
Rogers
Diffusion of Innovations · 1962
Qualitative and sociological. Describes who adopts and in what sequence — Innovators, Early Adopters, Early Majority, Late Majority, Laggards. Explains the decision process. Does not predict timing.
Model 02
Bass
Diffusion Model · 1969
Mathematical and quantitative. Models the cumulative adoption curve using innovation (p) and imitation (q) coefficients. Assumes gradual, continuous diffusion. Strong for forecasting consumer products.
Model 03
Zorthex
Diffusion Lag Framework · 2026
Empirical and descriptive. Measures the lag between when a phenomenon becomes operationally real and when public attention consolidates around it. 70 verified cases, three independent sources, modern digital proxies.

What Each Model Actually Measures

Rogers — the process inside

Rogers is concerned with the individual or organisational adoption decision. He asks: why do some people adopt earlier than others? What attributes of the innovation matter? How does the social system shape uptake? Rogers works from the inside out — his unit of analysis is the adopter.

Bass — the aggregate curve

Bass translates Rogers's intuition into a mathematical model. He asks: given historical adoption data, can we fit a curve and forecast future uptake? His model works well for consumer goods with clear sales data. It assumes that adoption spreads gradually through imitation, following a smooth S-curve.

Zorthex — the visibility gap

Zorthex asks a different question: when does a technology become visible to the public? Not when it is invented. Not when it is adopted. When it generates sustained, structural attention — measured through modern digital proxies: Google Trends (operational signal), Wikipedia pageviews (informational signal), Reddit (community proxy).

The metric is L = t_peak − t_start: the gap in months between first documented emergence and structural public attention consolidation. Across 70 verified cases, this gap averages years — and varies systematically by domain and regime.

The key distinction: Rogers and Bass study diffusion — adoption spreading through a population. Zorthex studies attention diffusion — awareness spreading through public consciousness. They are measuring different phenomena. Both matter. Neither replaces the other.

Where Zorthex Challenges Bass

The Bass model rests on an assumption of gradual diffusion — adoption spreads continuously through imitation, producing a smooth S-curve. This works well for consumer products. It breaks down for a specific class of technologies.

The Dual-Velocity challenge to Bass: In B2B, security, cryptographic infrastructure, and policy-driven domains, the diffusion pattern is not gradual. Specialist attention (Wikipedia) runs at sustained high levels for years — sometimes decades — while public search attention (Google Trends) stays near zero. Then, triggered by a regulatory event, public attention ignites in a single month. The S-curve never forms. The breakout is vertical.

Six Perspectives, One Gap

More than seventy frameworks have been proposed to explain technology diffusion. Here is where Zorthex fits relative to six of the most cited — and what each one leaves unmeasured.

Everett Rogers · Diffusion of Innovations (1962)
Classifies who adopts and in what sequence — Innovators to Laggards. Qualitative, sociological. Zorthex adds the temporal public dimension: how long before consolidated attention forms around what specialists already know.
Frank Bass · Bass Model (1969)
Predicts adoption curves mathematically via innovation and imitation coefficients. Assumes gradual diffusion. Dual-Velocity challenges this assumption for policy-driven domains — the curve is flat for years, then vertical in a single month.
Fred Davis · TAM (1989)
Measures why individuals accept or resist a technology — perceived usefulness and ease of use. Psychological, individual-level. Zorthex measures when the public sees it. Complementary, non-overlapping.
Geoffrey Moore · Crossing the Chasm (1991)
Describes the gap between early adopters and early majority — qualitatively. The Dual-Velocity pattern is the chasm in data form: Wikipedia high + Google Trends near zero is the chasm made visible. Zorthex measures empirically what Moore describes narratively.
Brian Arthur · Path Dependence (1989)
Explains why certain standards win through historical inertia, not merit — QWERTY, VHS. In the Zorthex dataset, the Market-Narrative regime is often path-dependent: the frame that arrives first at breakout tends to dominate the attention cycle.
Bruno Latour · Actor-Network Theory
Technology diffuses through networks of human and non-human actors. The regulatory trigger in Dual-Velocity is exactly this: a single actor that unblocks the entire network — explaining why four phenomena converge in six weeks.
The shared gap: Rogers, Bass, Moore, Davis, Arthur and Latour all study adoption dynamics, organisational behaviour, or sociotechnical networks. None of them measure when a phenomenon becomes publicly visible — the lag between operational reality and consolidated public attention. That is what Zorthex measures.

How the Models Fit Together

If you need to... Best model Why
Forecast sales or adoption volume Bass Mathematical formula, strong for consumer products
Understand which segments to target first Rogers / Moore Explains who adopts early and what drives the chasm
Understand individual resistance to adoption Davis (TAM) Perceived usefulness and ease of use at individual level
Know when a technology will become publicly visible Zorthex Measures the lag between operational reality and public attention
Navigate B2B, regulated, or policy-driven technologies Zorthex (Dual-Velocity) Shows that these often do not follow Bass's gradual curve
Build a complete picture All together Rogers + Bass + Moore = adoption dynamics. Davis = individual psychology. Arthur = path dependency. Latour = network dynamics. Zorthex = timing of public visibility.
