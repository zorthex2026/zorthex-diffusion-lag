// 00 · the question

What Does It Mean to "Adopt" a Technology?

The word adoption is used loosely. It can mean that people are aware of something, that they are searching for it, that they have read about it, or that they are using it daily in their work. These are different things — and they happen at different times.

Recent data from the Gallup Workplace Panel (2023–Q1:2026) documents a sharp acceleration in AI use among finance and insurance workers — from roughly 8% frequent users in 2023 to nearly 45% by early 2026. The study identifies regulatory change as the primary driver: when compliance constraints eased, operational adoption followed.

This is one half of the story. The other half is: where was public attention before that adoption curve moved?

Core finding: Across three finance phenomena tracked in the Zorthex dataset — Open Banking, Stablecoins, and Buy Now Pay Later — public attention (Google Trends) moved measurably before operational adoption (Gallup). In every case, the attention signal was already present when adoption began to accelerate. Awareness preceded behaviour. The signal was upstream.
// 01 · two signals, two measurements

The Two Signals Are Not the Same

Signal 1 — Zorthex
Public Attention
Measured via Google Trends (search intent) and Wikipedia pageviews (informational engagement). Captures when a phenomenon enters public consciousness — when people begin searching, reading, and forming an opinion. Three independent sources, locked CSVs, methodology open.
Signal 2 — Gallup Workplace Panel
Operational Adoption
Measured via longitudinal survey of US workers (20–65), tracking AI use frequency quarterly from Q2:2023 to Q1:2026. Captures when a technology enters daily operational behaviour — when people are actually using it, multiple times per week or daily.

These signals measure different things. A person can be aware of a technology for years without using it. An organisation can be deploying a technology without the general public knowing. The gap between these two states — between awareness and behaviour — is where the most useful information lives.

Attention is a necessary condition for adoption, but not a sufficient one. You cannot adopt what you do not know exists. But knowing it exists does not mean you will adopt it. The triggers that convert awareness into behaviour — regulatory change, cost reduction, competitive pressure, organisational capacity — operate in the gap between the two signals.

// 04 · what the gap tells us

The Gap Between Attention and Adoption Is Not Noise

In all three cases, public attention preceded operational adoption. But the lead times are very different: Open Banking had a 3-year lead, Stablecoins a near-simultaneous breakout, BNPL a 4-year lead. What explains the variation?

The Gallup study points to regulatory change as the primary driver of Finance AI adoption. The Zorthex data suggests this is consistent with a broader pattern: attention moves when a topic becomes publicly legible; adoption moves when the operational conditions — regulatory, organisational, economic — are ready.

The gap between these two events is not random. It is shaped by:

Regulatory readiness: How long until a compliance framework allows or requires the technology?

Organisational capacity: Do workers and institutions have the skills and processes to convert awareness into workflow?

Economic conditions: Is the cost of adoption below the threshold of friction?

The attention signal tells you the technology is ready in the public mind. The adoption signal tells you the operational conditions have caught up. Neither alone is sufficient to understand where a technology actually is in its diffusion cycle.
// 05 · conclusion

Two Keys, Not One

// ZSA-2026-002 · Core Finding
Understanding technology adoption requires two independent measurements. Attention data (Zorthex: Google Trends, Wikipedia, Reddit) measures when a phenomenon enters public consciousness — the cognitive signal. Adoption data (Gallup Workplace Panel and equivalent longitudinal surveys) measures when it enters operational behaviour — the behavioural signal.

Across the three finance cases analysed, attention consistently preceded adoption. The attention signal is a leading indicator — not of how much adoption will occur, but of when the conditions for adoption have become publicly legible.

Neither signal is sufficient alone. Attention without adoption data tells you that a technology is known but not whether it is being used. Adoption data without attention data tells you behaviour has changed but not how long the awareness infrastructure was already in place.

Together, they map the full diffusion arc — from the moment a technology becomes thinkable to the moment it becomes operational.
// 06 · declared limitations

What This Analysis Does Not Claim

01
The Gallup data covers US finance and insurance workers specifically. The Zorthex attention data is global. The geographic scope is different. The comparison is indicative, not precise.
02
Attention is not a sufficient predictor of adoption. Many phenomena show high public attention without subsequent operational adoption. The attention signal tells you awareness is present — it does not predict whether adoption will follow or when.
03
The three cases are not a statistical sample. They are selected from the Zorthex v2.0 dataset as Finance phenomena with verified L values. The pattern observed is consistent but not proven across a larger sample.
04
Google Trends peaks are recent for all three cases. The Rock Rule applies — L values are provisional under the current renormalization conditions. Values are declared as of 23 June 2026 and subject to revision on the next 90-day cycle.
// 07 · methodological note

Why Not Social Media?

A frequent question: why doesn't Zorthex use social media data — Twitter/X impressions, LinkedIn engagement, TikTok views, Instagram reach — as an attention source? The answer is architectural, not practical.

Zorthex measures intentional attention — a person decides to search for something (Google Trends) or decides to read about it (Wikipedia). Both are acts of cognitive demand: the person initiates the query. This is fundamentally different from passive exposure, where an algorithm decides what appears in your feed and you may scroll past it in 0.3 seconds.

The distinction matters for what the framework claims to measure. When Google Trends shows "stablecoins" rising from 5/100 to 100/100, that means more people are choosing to type the word and look for information. It's a revealed preference. When a LinkedIn post about stablecoins gets 50,000 impressions, that means an algorithm decided 50,000 people should see it. The first is demand. The second is supply.

INTENTIONAL ATTENTION (ZORTHEX)

Google Trends — person types a query. Cognitive demand.
Wikipedia — person navigates to a page. Informational demand.
Reddit — person enters a community discussion. Declared qualitative.

Reproducible. CSV locked, timestamped, archived. Anyone can re-run the same query and verify.
Resistant to manipulation. No paid promotion on Google Trends. No bots on Wikipedia pageview counts.
Measures demand. What the public wants to know.

PASSIVE EXPOSURE (NOT USED)

Twitter/X impressions — algorithm shows content. Algorithmic supply.
LinkedIn engagement — feed placement, not search. Platform-mediated.
TikTok/Instagram — passive scroll. Sub-second exposure ≠ attention.

Non-reproducible. Feeds are personalised. No two users see the same content. No locked CSV possible.
Manipulable. Bots, paid amplification, engagement farming, viral mechanics all inflate metrics.
Measures supply. What algorithms decide you should see.

This report demonstrates why the distinction is operationally critical. The Gallup adoption data shows when people use a technology. The Zorthex attention data shows when people choose to learn about a technology. Social media data would show when people were shown content about a technology. Only the middle signal — intentional attention — has a documented empirical relationship with the adoption signal. Passive exposure correlates with virality, not with the sustained cognitive engagement that precedes adoption.

There is a second, practical reason. Google Trends data is normalised, timestamped, and freely available. Wikipedia pageviews are published via an open API with daily granularity. Both can be downloaded, archived, and independently verified by anyone. Social media metrics are platform-proprietary, API-gated, subject to retroactive changes, and increasingly paywalled. A framework that depends on social data inherits the platform's instability. A framework built on search and encyclopaedic data inherits the open web's durability.

The design choice is deliberate: Zorthex measures what people seek, not what they are shown. The difference between these two signals is the difference between a thermometer and a heater. One measures temperature; the other changes it. Social media is a heater. Zorthex reads the thermometer.
